# The Herald Family

You're reading the docs for **memex**, the memory layer of a small family of local-first tools. They share one principle: **your data stays on your machine, and the AI comes to it** — no uploads, no cloud, no account.

## Three tools, one stack

| Tool | What it is | What it owns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **memex** — *you are here* | persistent memory | your notes and the context behind them, across every session |
| **firma** | financial intelligence | portfolio · net worth · cash flow, in `~/.firma/firma.db` |
| **skope** | news intelligence | a personalized lens on the world — the news that reaches *you* |

Each is an independent MCP server you install on its own. None depends on the others — but Claude can hold all three at once, and that's where they compound: memex remembers the decisions you made in firma last month, and recalls why you started following a story skope first surfaced.

## Federation, not lock-in

The tools never call each other. Each owns its own data and exposes its own MCP tools; Claude is the only thing that sees across them. Add or drop any one and the rest keep working. That's the whole design — **compose through the model, not through a backend.**

## Explore the rest of the family

### firma — your money

Drop your trade history into Claude and get the full picture — portfolio, net worth, cash flow — in one local SQLite file. No account, no cloud.

[GitHub →](https://github.com/evan-moon/firma) · `npm install -g @evan-moon/firma`

### skope — your news

A lens **and** a watcher on the world. Claude scans the news, skope keeps only what has a causal path to you, and warns you when your attention collapses into a bubble.

[GitHub →](https://github.com/evan-moon/skope) · `npm install -g @evan-moon/skope`
